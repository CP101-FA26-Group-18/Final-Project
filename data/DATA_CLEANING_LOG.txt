ACS DRAFT (TO BE COMPLETELY REWRITTEN)

The American Community Survey (ACS) county-level work-from-home (WFH) dataset was constructed from Table B08301 (Means of Transportation to Work for Workers 16 Years and Over) across the nine Bay Area counties for 2017–2019 and 2021–2023. The 2020 cycle was excluded because the Census Bureau suspended standard 1-year ACS data releases due to pandemic-related collection disruptions. To clean the raw Census tables, descriptive metadata header rows were removed by filtering out entries where `GEO_ID` equaled `'Geography'`, all margin-of-error columns ending in `'M'` were dropped via regex filtering, and 14-digit Census geographic identifiers were sliced to their terminal five characters to extract standard five-digit county FIPS codes (`06001` through `06097`). These FIPS codes were then mapped to their respective county names using a standardized dictionary.

Countywide WFH proportions were computed by dividing worked-from-home counts (`B08301_021E`) by the total worker universe (`B08301_001E`). Because Napa County (`06055`) fell below Census publication thresholds for single-year ACS products in 2021 and 2022, missing entries were preserved as `NaN` rather than imputed to avoid introducing artificial distortion into the longitudinal panel. The resulting multi-year panel was exported to `data/processed/ACS_WFH.csv`. The cleaned data reflects pre-pandemic WFH proportions ranging from 4.04% in Napa to 15.44% in Marin between 2017 and 2019, peaking in 2021 between 12.76% in Solano and 45.58% in San Francisco, and stabilizing by 2023 at an elevated hybrid baseline between 11.12% in Solano and 26.27% in Marin.
